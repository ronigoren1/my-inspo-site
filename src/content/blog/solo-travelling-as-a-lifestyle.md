as the year comes to an end, and with my 23rd birthday coming up, I decided to treat myself to yet another solo trip to a city I've never been to before - Madrid, this year.

my birthday is at the very end of November, right before Christmas - and even though I didn't grow up celebrating it, it's easily my favorite time of the year. the crisp air, the early evening lights, the quiet energy of a city getting ready for winter.

and now, two weeks before my flight, I sat down to write about a wonderful thing called solo travelling. for those of you who have yet to try it, I'm here to share a few angles on why I think it's so necessary to experience. for those who have tried it before - I am here to share the best tips on how to make it the most awesome experience.

## the best thing about it is the complete lack of compromise.

in everyday life, we are constantly adjusting our rhythm to other people - when to wake up, where to eat, how fast to walk, what we're supposed to be doing next. when you travel alone, all of that background noise disappears. you get to move entirely at your own pace and meet yourself without an audience.

there is also a quiet kind of confidence that comes from knowing you can land in a brand new city, figure out the streets, sit at a corner table by yourself, and feel completely at home. once you prove to yourself that you can rely on your own company anywhere in the world, everything back home feels a little less intimidating.

and honestly, you just see more when you're alone. when you're with someone else, half of your attention is always inside the conversation. when it's just you, you actually notice the details - the architecture, the way late afternoon light hits an old stone building, the local bookstores tucked down side streets, the sound of live jazz spilling out of a doorway at night.

if you want to make a solo trip feel less like a checklist and more like a lifestyle, here are a few things I swear by -

**keep your morning anchor.** don't throw your routine out the window just because you're away. wake up early, move your body, get a workout in or go for a long walk before the streets get crowded. starting the day grounded makes everything else feel effortless.

**bring a real book and a notebook.** put the phone away and leave the headphones in your bag for a few hours. sitting in a classic cafe with a good book or writing down your thoughts in a physical journal turns a simple coffee break into a scene out of an old movie. you're never really alone when you have good pages with you.

![](solo-travel-book.jpg)

**find your regular spot.** instead of rushing to try a different trendy place every single time, pick one neighborhood cafe or bakery and go back two or three mornings in a row. there is something really comforting about having a familiar table in a brand new city.

![](solo-travel-coffee.jpg)

**dress up for nobody.** put on your favorite coat, take yourself out to a proper dinner, sit at a dimly lit bar with good music. romanticizing your own company is half the magic of being away.

**leave room for nothing.** pick one or two things you genuinely care about each day - a street of vintage books, a quiet gallery, a viewpoint right before sunset - and leave the rest of the day open. the best moments always happen when you let yourself wander without a strict schedule.

> solo travelling isn't about running away from people - it's about liking your own company enough to take yourself somewhere new.

two weeks until Madrid, and I literally can't wait.
